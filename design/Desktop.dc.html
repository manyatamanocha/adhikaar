The rule, in the RBI's own words

“… the bank shall not insist on production of legal documents such as Succession Certificate, Letter of Administration, Probate of Will, etc., or seek any bond of indemnity/ surety … irrespective of the amount standing to the credit.”

Paragraph 9, applying where a nominee or surviving joint holder is on record. RBI/2025-26/82 · issued 26 September 2025

The rule changed. Most of the internet has not caught up.

The Reserve Bank has issued this instruction three times — 2005, January 2013, and September 2025. A rule reissued three times in twenty years is one that is not reaching the counter.

What a search usually tells you

  • Claimable only through a succession certificate
  • A petition before a District Judge
  • Notice to every heir, and a newspaper advertisement
  • Roughly ₹17,000, and four to seven months

What the Directions actually say

  • With a nominee or survivor on record, a bank must not insist at all — whatever the amount
  • No succession certificate, probate or administration
  • No indemnity bond, and no third-party surety
  • With no nominee, a simplified procedure below the floor

What this does not cover

Bank deposits, lockers and safe custody only. Insurance, mutual funds, shares, provident fund, pension and post office savings run on their own rails.
